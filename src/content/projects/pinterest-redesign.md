## Brief and role

As a regular Pinterest user, I wanted to explore how the mobile app could support inspiration and content discovery with less visual distraction. This self-initiated, unofficial redesign took me from an exploratory survey and personas through wireframes to high-fidelity interface design in Figma. It is a portfolio concept, not a change to the live Pinterest product.

## Research and its limits

I used a Google Forms survey to ask people about their experience with Pinterest: what they liked, what frustrated them, and what they would change. I combined those responses with my own experience to develop two lightweight personas.

Janet represents someone who saves pins and browses recommended content for artistic inspiration but struggles to distinguish promoted content from organic posts. Matthew represents someone who collects and organises ideas into folders and wants to retrieve them without unnecessary visual distraction.

The survey informed the direction, but the case study does not document its recruitment method or establish that respondents represent Pinterest’s wider audience. My own preferences also influenced the interpretation. The personas are design tools, not evidence that all users share these needs.

## Problem framing

The case study identifies three themes: difficulty recognising ads, a perception that chat was unnecessary, and a desire for more focus and less clutter. I used these themes to ask how the interface could make browsing and organising ideas feel more direct.

These were priorities for this concept, not proof that the existing features were universally problematic.

## Design decisions and tradeoffs

In the low-fidelity wireframes, I simplified the interface, emphasised visual hierarchy, and explored more direct navigation. I made promoted content more clearly identifiable so that the distinction between advertising and organic inspiration would be easier to notice.

I also removed in-app chat to focus the concept on discovering, saving, and organising ideas. This reduces the number of functions competing for attention, but it is a deliberate tradeoff: people who use Pinterest to share ideas or collaborate through messages would lose that route. The survey does not establish that removing chat would benefit the wider user base. Retaining messaging in a less prominent location would be an alternative worth comparing.

## High-fidelity design and deliverable

I translated the wireframes into high-fidelity screens, keeping the visual direction minimal and focused on content. The final concept includes browsing, search, profile, board, and pin-related screens, with clearer treatment of promoted content and navigation centred on discovery and organisation.

The linked Figma prototype and visual case study show the proposed interface. The case study does not document usability testing of the redesign, so greater clarity, easier navigation, and reduced distraction remain design intentions rather than demonstrated improvements.

## Reflection and next steps

The project gave me practice connecting early research to interface decisions and presenting a complete redesign concept. It also highlighted the risk of treating my own browsing habits as a default for everyone.

A next step would be to compare the concept with the existing experience using tasks around identifying promoted content, saving ideas, and finding saved pins. Research should include people who use messaging as well as those who do not, so the cost of removing chat can be evaluated rather than assumed away.
