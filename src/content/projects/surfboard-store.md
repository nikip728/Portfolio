## Project Background

This project was my introduction to UI/UX design through the Udemy course “Figma UI UX Design Essentials,” where each student received a randomly generated design brief. Mine was to create a surfboard store with a trendy visual direction.

The goal was to practise the fundamentals of UI design in Figma, translating the brief into a cohesive shopping experience across mobile and desktop screens.

## Visual Research & Inspiration

As this was an introductory UI-focused exercise, I did not conduct formal user or competitor research. Instead, I gathered visual references from Pinterest and Google, organising them into a moodboard to establish the overall direction.

## Structure & Wireframing

I started with low-fidelity wireframes to establish the layout and structure before moving on to high-fidelity designs.

The store included a homepage, product pages, shopping cart, about page, reviews, navigation menu, order tracking and user profile, covering the main sections of an e-commerce experience.

## Visual Design & Design System

My main visual decision was the colour palette. I chose pastel green and pink to give the surfboard store a playful feel, with subtle wave-inspired details to reflect the theme.

I created reusable elements, including the logo, buttons, and icons, to keep the interface consistent. I organised them in a dedicated design system in Figma, keeping copies of their original versions separate to avoid accidental modifications.

![A screenshot of the components page.](/images/projects/surfboard-store/surfing-app-components.png)

## Responsive Design & Prototyping

I adapted the designs for mobile and MacBook-sized desktop screens. On desktop, I used side-by-side and grid layouts, with horizontal scrolling mainly used for product lists.

The About Us page shows how I adapted the same content for different screen sizes. On mobile, I stacked the sections vertically and alternated the image placement. On desktop, I arranged them as a horizontal row of cards, with each image above its description.

I connected the individual screens into a clickable Figma prototype, bringing the store together as a navigable experience.

## Conclusion & Reflection

This project introduced me to the fundamentals of UI design and helped me become comfortable working with Figma. I gained practical experience in wireframing, high-fidelity design, component organisation, responsive layouts and prototyping.

Looking back, I would include some lightweight user and competitor research if I revisited the project today. I am particularly proud of the clickable prototype, as it brought all the individual screens together into one cohesive experience.
